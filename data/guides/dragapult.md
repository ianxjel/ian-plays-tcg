Dragapult is the most-played deck in the format and the one every other deck is built to answer. It is slow to start and hard to stop: two turns of setup, then 200 damage to the Active and 60 more to the bench every turn. This guide teaches it from the list up.

## At a glance

- **Plays like:** a Stage 2 engine. Turn one and two are for Dreepy, Drakloak and Energy; from turn three Dragapult ex attacks every turn.
- **Wins by:** turning bench damage into prizes. Phantom Dive's 6 damage counters and Munkidori's 3 build Knock Outs on Pokémon that never had to be Active.
- **Prize profile:** Dragapult ex, Meowth ex and Fezandipiti ex give up 2. Everything else gives up 1.
- **Fastest attack:** your third turn. The list has no Rare Candy, so it is always Dreepy, then Drakloak, then Dragapult ex.
- **What beats it:** Lillie's Clefairy ex (gives your Dragons Psychic Weakness), Pokémon with Hide 'n' Sneak (they ignore the counters), and Battle Cage (no counters on the bench at all).

## The list

## Card by card

### The Dragapult line

- **Dreepy (70 HP).** Your opener. Its attacks don't matter; what matters is having two or three on the bench on turn one so two of them can evolve on turn two.
- **Drakloak (90 HP).** Recon Directive: once a turn, look at the top 2 cards, keep 1, put the other on the bottom. Two Drakloak means two looks a turn, which is how a deck with only 4 Lillie's Determination stays consistent. Drakloak is also your most exposed piece; at 90 HP it is the favorite target of every snipe in the format.
- **Dragapult ex (320 HP, no Weakness).** Phantom Dive costs Fire and Psychic and does 200 to the Active, then puts 6 damage counters on the opponent's benched Pokémon in any split. Jet Headbutt does 70 for one Energy, useful when a Hammer or a Knock Out leaves you short. Dragapult ex is a Tera Pokémon: while it sits on your bench it takes no damage from attacks. Damage counters placed by effects still land on it.

### Support Pokémon

- **Munkidori (110 HP).** With any Darkness Energy attached, Adrena-Brain moves up to 3 damage counters from one of your Pokémon to one of theirs, once a turn. It needs damage on your side to work. Early it does nothing; after they hit your Dragapult ex, it heals 30 and deals 30 in one move.
- **Budew (30 HP).** Itchy Pollen costs no Energy: 10 damage, and your opponent can't play Item cards on their next turn. Going second, it locks Items before they have played a single one.
- **Dunsparce and Dudunsparce.** Run Away Draw draws 3 and shuffles Dudunsparce back into your deck, so the same copy can come back later.
- **Meowth ex.** When you play it onto your bench, Last-Ditch Catch searches your deck for a Supporter. It is a Supporter search that gives up 2 prizes if it gets stuck on the bench, so bench it when you need the Supporter, not before.
- **Fezandipiti ex.** Flip the Script draws 3 if one of your Pokémon was Knocked Out on their last turn. Cruel Arrow (three Energy of any type) does 100 to any of their Pokémon, which finishes a benched target that Phantom Dive has already softened.

### Trainers

- **Crispin** finds two Basic Energy of different types, attaches one and puts the other in hand. Fire plus Psychic is a whole Phantom Dive cost in one card.
- **Crushing Hammer** discards an Energy from any of their Pokémon on heads. Four copies is a commitment: on average two heads, which is two turns of tempo against decks that need three Energy to attack.
- **Risky Ruins** puts 2 damage counters on every Basic non-Darkness Pokémon a player puts onto their bench during their turn. Yours too, but Munkidori can move those counters onto them. It is also your answer to their Stadiums: playing it discards Battle Cage, Area Zero Underdepths or Legendary Pyroduct.
- **Unfair Stamp** only works after you lose a Pokémon. Both players shuffle their hands in; you draw 5, they draw 2.
- **Lillie's Determination** draws 8 instead of 6 while you still have all 6 prize cards, so it is strongest early.

### Energy

Three of each type. Fire and Psychic pay for Phantom Dive; Darkness turns on Munkidori. Crispin can fetch any two of the three, so think about which pair you need before you play it.

## The first two turns

Your goal for turns one and two is the same every game: two Drakloak by the end of turn two, at least one of them already holding a Fire and a Psychic, and a Darkness on Munkidori.

### Going second

1. Start Budew if you can. Itchy Pollen on turn one means no Ultra Ball, no Energy Switch, no Poffin for them on their second turn.
2. Buddy-Buddy Poffin for two Dreepy (both are 70 HP or less). Poké Pad finds a third Dreepy or Munkidori.
3. Crispin for Fire and Psychic: attach one to a Dreepy, then attach the other from hand. That Dreepy now holds the full Phantom Dive cost two turns early.
4. On turn two, evolve two Dreepy and use Recon Directive on both before you choose your Supporter. Itchy Pollen again if Budew is still Active.

### Going first

You can't attack or play a Supporter on turn one, so Budew does nothing yet. Bench three Dreepy if you can (Poffin plus Poké Pad), attach an Energy, and keep Budew in hand for turn two, when Itchy Pollen stops their turn-three Items.

> If your opening hand has no Dreepy and no way to find one, bench whatever Basics you must and use Lillie's Determination at 6 prizes for 8 cards. A slow Dragapult turn is normal; a Dragapult turn with no Dreepy on the bench is how games are lost.

## The middle game: placing counters

From turn three, every Phantom Dive is two decisions: what the 200 hits, and where 6 counters go. The second decision wins more games.

### Plan Knock Outs two turns ahead

A benched Pokémon with 60 damage on it is 60 HP closer to dying when you Boss's Orders it next turn. Useful thresholds:

| Target | HP | Counters to KO it from the bench |
|---|---|---|
| Budew, Poltchageist | 30 | 3 |
| Abra | 50 | 5 |
| Dreepy, Dunsparce, Drilbur | 70 | 7 (one Dive plus Munkidori) |
| Slowpoke | 80 | 8 (one Dive plus Munkidori) |
| Drakloak | 90 | 9 (one Dive plus Munkidori) |
| Metang | 100 | 10 |
| N's Zekrom | 130 | 13 |
| Meowth ex | 170 | 17 |

Two thresholds matter most: 9 counters (one Phantom Dive plus one Munkidori) kills any 90 HP Stage 1 in a single turn, and two Dives plus Munkidori (15 counters) kill most single-prize support.

### Spread versus focus

Put all 6 on one Pokémon when that finishes it this turn or next. Split them when a 2 or 3 split sets up a double Knock Out next turn: for example 3 on Budew (a KO now) and 3 on a Pokémon you will Boss later.

### Use Munkidori after they hit you

Munkidori's 3 counters come off your own Pokémon. The best moment is the turn after they hit Dragapult ex without Knocking it Out: you heal 30 and add 30 to a target at the same time.

### Hammer what pays for the attack

Crushing Hammer is best on the Energy that pays their attack cost, not just any Energy. Against Mega Rayquaza ex, every Fire or Lightning anywhere on their side is 50 damage, so any heads helps; against Mega Excadrill ex, taking one Energy from 5 drops Maximum Drilling from 330 to 200.

## Closing the game

- **Count prizes in Knock Outs, not cards.** Against decks built on Megas (Mega Rayquaza ex, Mega Kangaskhan ex, Mega Excadrill ex) two Knock Outs on Megas are all 6 prizes.
- **Boss's Orders is your finisher.** Save at least one for the turn a damaged benched Pokémon is your last prize or two.
- **Fezandipiti ex's Cruel Arrow** (100 to any Pokémon) finishes a benched target when Dragapult ex can't attack.
- **Watch your own bench late.** Every benched Pokémon is another target for their Boss's Orders or snipes. Late in the game, bench only what you need.

## Decisions that decide games

1. **Start Budew or Dreepy?** Budew if you are going second and have a Dreepy to bench; Dreepy if you are going first and have no Poffin.
2. **When to play Risky Ruins.** Play it when they are about to bench several Basics, or to remove their Stadium. Hold it while you still need to bench three Dreepy yourself.
3. **Which Drakloak becomes Dragapult first.** The one already holding Fire and Psychic. A Dragapult ex with no Energy is just a 320 HP wall.
4. **Bench size against Lillie's Clefairy ex.** Every Pokémon on either bench adds 40 to Full Moon Rondo against your Dragons. Bench only what you need.
5. **Unfair Stamp timing.** Best when their hand is big and yours is weak, right after they take a Knock Out.

## Playthroughs

These are worked examples built from the cards' text, not records of real games. Numbers are exact unless marked as an estimate.

### Going second into Mega Rayquaza

```play
setup: You open Budew Active with Dreepy A on the bench. They go first: Mega Kangaskhan ex Active, Mega Rayquaza ex benched (Ruler's Roar attaches a Lightning), and a Fire attached. Fire and Lightning on their side: 2.
you 1: Buddy-Buddy Poffin benches Dreepy B. Crispin: Psychic onto Dreepy A, Fire to hand; attach that Fire to Dreepy A too. It now holds the whole Phantom Dive cost. Budew uses Itchy Pollen: no Items for them next turn.
opp 2: No Energy Switch, no Ultra Ball. They attach a Lightning to Rayquaza (count 3), bench Latias ex so their Basics retreat for free, swap Rayquaza in and Storm Emerald Budew for 150. They take 1 prize.
you 2: Promote Dreepy B, keeping Dreepy A safe on the bench. Evolve both into Drakloak and use Recon Directive twice. Bench Munkidori and attach Darkness. Two Crushing Hammers, one heads: discard the Fire on Rayquaza. Without a Fire on Rayquaza they can't pay Storm Emerald until they attach another.
opp 3: They attach a Fire to Rayquaza (count 3) and Storm Emerald Drakloak B for 150. They have taken 2 prizes.
you 3: Promote Drakloak A and evolve it into Dragapult ex. Phantom Dive: 200 to Rayquaza (80 left) and 6 counters on the benched Kangaskhan (240 left). The third Dreepy, benched last turn with Poké Pad, evolves into Drakloak and gets a Psychic.
opp 4: They attach a Lightning to the benched Kangaskhan, which still counts: Storm Emerald counts Energy on all their Pokémon (count 4). Storm Emerald hits Dragapult ex for 200. It survives with 120 HP left: 320 HP is more than any Storm Emerald below 7 Fire and Lightning.
you 4: Munkidori moves 3 counters from Dragapult ex to Rayquaza: Dragapult ex is back to 150, Rayquaza has 50 left. Phantom Dive Knocks Out Rayquaza: 3 prizes. The 6 counters go on Kangaskhan (180 left). Attach Fire to the third Drakloak.
opp 5: Kangaskhan's Rapid-Fire Combo Knocks Out Dragapult ex. They have taken 4 prizes.
you 5: Evolve the third Drakloak (Fire and Psychic attached) into Dragapult ex. Phantom Dive does 200 to Kangaskhan, which had 180 left. 3 more prizes: you win.
lesson: Against Megas you need only two Knock Outs. Spend the early turns keeping their Fire and Lightning count under 7 with Budew and Hammers so Dragapult ex survives a hit, then let Munkidori turn the damage they did to you into the last 30.
```

### Into Basic Box: kill Clefairy before it swings

```play
setup: You go first. They open Teal Mask Ogerpon ex. Lillie's Clefairy ex's Fairy Zone gives your Dragon Pokémon Psychic Weakness, and Full Moon Rondo does 20 plus 20 for every benched Pokémon on both sides. Against Dragapult ex that is doubled: it Knocks Out a 320 HP Dragapult ex once 7 Pokémon are benched in total.
you 1: Dreepy A Active, Dreepy B and C benched with Poffin, Psychic attached to Dreepy B. No Supporter on the first turn going first.
opp 1: Teal Dance attaches Grass to Ogerpon and draws. They bench Mega Kangaskhan ex, Meowth ex (Last-Ditch Catch finds Crispin) and Lillie's Clefairy ex. Three benched.
you 2: Evolve two Dreepy, Recon Directive twice. Bench Munkidori with Darkness. Play Risky Ruins: every Basic they bench from now on takes 2 counters. Attach Fire to Drakloak B, which now holds Fire and Psychic. You have three benched; total is now 6.
opp 2: They play Area Zero Underdepths (Ogerpon is a Tera Pokémon, so they may bench up to 8), replacing Risky Ruins, and bench Fezandipiti ex and Latias ex. Ogerpon's Myriad Leaf Shower Knocks Out your Active Drakloak. Total benched: 5 theirs plus 2 yours is 7, exactly what Clefairy needs.
you 3: Drakloak B, promoted after the Knock Out, evolves into Dragapult ex. Boss's Orders Lillie's Clefairy ex (190 HP) and Phantom Dive it for 200: 2 prizes. Put the 6 counters on Meowth ex (170 HP).
opp 3: Their second Clefairy is not in play yet. Ogerpon hits Dragapult ex but can't Knock it Out this turn (an estimate; it depends on how much Energy they have attached).
you 4: Munkidori moves 3 counters from Dragapult ex to Meowth ex (90 on it now). Phantom Dive the Active, and 6 more counters on Meowth ex (150). If the second Clefairy comes down, it is the next Boss's Orders target.
lesson: Count benched Pokémon on both sides every turn; you control your half. Then remove Lillie's Clefairy ex the turn it appears: at 190 HP it dies to one Phantom Dive, and nothing else in Basic Box threatens a full-HP Dragapult ex as cheaply.
```

### Into N's Zoroark: take the Zekrom away

```play
setup: N's Zoroark ex's Night Joker copies an attack from one of its benched N's Pokémon. The list plays 2 N's Zekrom (130 HP); Rampaging Thunder is the 250-damage attack it copies. No benched Zekrom means no big attack.
you 3: Your first Dragapult ex attacks. Phantom Dive does 200 to the Active N's Zoroark ex (80 left) and all 6 counters go on the benched N's Zekrom (70 left).
opp 3: Pecharunt ex's Subjugating Chains switches in a fresh Zoroark and Poisons it, which turns on Binding Mochi (+40). Night Joker copies Rampaging Thunder: 290 to Dragapult ex. It survives with 30 left. That Zoroark can't attack next turn.
you 4: Munkidori moves 3 counters from Dragapult ex onto Zekrom (40 left); Dragapult ex goes up to 60. Phantom Dive the Active Zoroark (280 to 80) and put 4 counters on Zekrom, which is Knocked Out, and 2 on the damaged Zoroark on the bench. You take 1 prize, and they have no Zekrom in play.
opp 4: With no Zekrom on their bench, Night Joker has nothing big to copy until they find the second one (or Night Stretcher the first) and bench it, and a fresh Zekrom on the bench is where your next 6 counters go.
lesson: The Zoroark deck's damage lives on a 130 HP benched Basic. Counters on N's Zekrom are worth more than counters anywhere else, and Munkidori finishes it with the damage they did to you.
```
